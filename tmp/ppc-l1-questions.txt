Track 1
The Gospel: The Word of Truth


Section A: MCQs


1. Why does the gospel's claim to be "the word of truth" require more than simply telling people to believe it?
A. Because truth can only be established by religious tradition
B. Because truth exists independently of whether people accept it and must therefore be examined and established
C. Because every person must have the same personal experience before believing
D. Because the gospel is mainly a philosophical argument


2. According to the teaching, which method can be used to establish truth about what, how, when, where, and who, while questions about why ultimately require revelation?
A. Observation and reason
B. Personal experience and tradition
C. Faith and religious authority
D. Emotion and conscience


3. What are the two primary conclusions derived from observing and reasoning about the world?
A. That humanity is naturally good and that the world will eventually improve
B. That there must be a God, and that there is a contradiction in the world that requires God's resolution
C. That science can explain everything and that revelation is unnecessary
D. That suffering proves God does not exist and that humanity must save itself


4. Why does the contradiction in the world point toward God as the necessary resolution?
A. Because every problem in the world can be solved through human knowledge
B. Because the source of reality cannot itself be deficient, so the contradiction must ultimately be resolved by the origin of all things
C. Because suffering is evidence that the world has no purpose
D. Because human beings are incapable of making moral decisions


5. What is the significance of Christ's death and resurrection in relation to the gospel's argument?
A. They provide a historical demonstration that God has resolved the contradiction of sin, death, and darkness through Christ
B. They prove that observation and reason are unnecessary for understanding the gospel
C. They show that the natural world is fundamentally evil
D. They establish that salvation comes through human moral improvement


Section B: SAQs


1. How do observation and reason lead to the conclusion that there must be a God?
Marking Scheme (1 mark):
The finite and contingent nature of the world requires an adequate cause, which the teaching identifies as God. (1)


2. How does the gospel present God's resolution of the world's contradiction?
Marking Scheme (1 mark):
The gospel presents Christ's death and resurrection as God's resolution of sin, death, and darkness, restoring humanity to God's purpose. (1)


Section C: Answer Key and Marking Guide


MCQs:
1. B
2. A
3. B
4. B
5. A

B. Because the purpose of an intelligent Creator cannot be known merely through observation and reasoning

A. Because God never gives personal guidance to believers
B. Because personal revelation is more important than Scripture
C. Because personal ideas about purpose can become subjective and contradict God's objectively revealed purpose
D. Because believers are not permitted to make personal decisions


3. According to Ephesians 1:5 and 1:9, what is God's purpose, described as His will and good pleasure?

B. The establishment of the church

A. That believers are prophets and teachers
B. That believers are children of God and heirs of God
C. That believers are saved from suffering and death
D. That believers are called to preach and evangelize


5. What does being an heir of God imply about a believer's purpose?
A. The believer is free from responsibility because everything has already been given to him
B. The believer has an assigned portion or responsibility, including natural and spiritual assignments to fulfill
C. The believer's only assignment is to attend church and pray
D. The believer's purpose is limited to finding a successful career


Section B: SAQs


1. Why must God's purpose for human existence be established from God's revealed Word rather than personal revelation?
Marking Scheme (1 mark):
God's purpose must be established from His revealed Word because it is God's objective revelation and provides the standard that personal revelation must not contradict. (1)


2. What is God's purpose according to Ephesians 1?
Marking Scheme (1 mark):
God's purpose is to bring humanity into sonship as His children through Christ, according to His good pleasure and will. (1)

The inward man is spiritually made new at salvation, while the outward man, including the physical body, remains subject to natural realities. (1)


2. What are the three major implications of the new creation?
Marking Scheme (1 mark):
The new creation gives the believer a new status, character, and abilities in Christ. (1)

B. The effort to practice moral instructions
C. The response of continuing versus misreckoning
D. The ability to recall scriptural statements


5. What is the correct sequence for spiritual development presented in the teaching?
A. Walk → Growth → Nature
B. Growth → Nature → Walk
C. Nature → Growth → Walk
D. Knowledge → Walk → Growth


Section B: SAQs


1. Explain the relationship between being a new creation and the instruction to walk in the Spirit.
Marking Scheme (4 marks):


        •        New creation is a reality at faith (not an instruction) (1)
        •        Receiving the Spirit / sealing with the Spirit (1)
        •        Walking is an instruction, not automatic (1)
        •        Walking depends on growth (1)


2. Define the process of growth as presented in the manuscript.
Marking Scheme (6 marks):


        •        Knowledge of the word / implanted word (2)
        •        Reckoning (logizomai – taking into account identity) (1)
        •        Continuing (not forgetting / abiding) (1)
        •        Presenting (paristemi – standing beside identity) (1)
        •        Renewing of the mind (1)


3. Explain the role of the mind in walking in the new creation.
Marking Scheme (4 marks):


        •        Mind determines walk (not nature) (1)
        •        Futility/ignorance vs knowledge (1)
        •        Renewing of the mind leads to transformation (1)
        •        Consciousness/awareness of identity (not forgetful) (1)

Section A: MCQs

A. Through disciplined reflection on experience
B. Through observation and rational deduction
C. Through revelation of His will
D. Through participation in spiritual activities

B. Will determines purpose, independent of good pleasure
C. Good pleasure, will, and purpose describe the same determined reality
D. Purpose emerges after human response to God’s will


3. What does the term “prothesis” (purpose) primarily convey?
A. A moral resolve to avoid failure
B. A fixed goal set before a person to pursue
C. A general desire to follow God
D. A response to external instruction


4. How is the believer’s life described in relation to purpose?
A. A continuous experience of inner peace
B. A gradual improvement of moral behavior
C. A race with a goal set before, to be pursued and finished
D. A balance between devotion and daily living


5. What constitutes the discipline required to fulfill purpose?
A. Consistency in religious practices
B. Awareness of spiritual identity
C. Definite goal, submission to order, and sustained drive
D. Emotional commitment to spiritual growth


Section B: SAQs


1. Explain the doctrinal meaning of God’s purpose as revealed in Ephesians 1.
Marking Scheme (4 marks):


        •        Purpose = will (what God wants) (1)
        •        Purpose = good pleasure (what God likes) (1)
        •        Revealed as mystery (known only by revelation) (1)
        •        Expressed as sonship (huiothesia) (1)


2. Define discipline (egkrateuomai / egkrateia) in relation to fulfilling purpose.
Marking Scheme (6 marks):


        •        Self-control / temperance (1)
        •        Definite goal (purpose set before) (1)
        •        Submission to order required to fulfill goal (2)
        •        Drive (intentional pursuit / slave-driving oneself) (2)


3. State and explain the three commitments required to fulfill purpose.
Marking Scheme (6 marks):


        •        Commitment to local church (order and participation) (2)
        •        Commitment to devotion (prayer, word, growth) (2)
        •        Commitment to assignments (natural and spiritual tasks) (2)
